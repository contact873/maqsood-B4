01
Work in Japan

Japan needs workers. The paperwork decides who arrives.

The labour shortage is not a slogan, it is policy. Japan has opened specific industries to foreign workers under the Specified Skilled Worker route, and receives technical intern trainees under the TIT route. Both are lawful, both are documented, and both require preparation before anyone leaves home.

ROUTE A

Specified Skilled Worker

For workers with demonstrated skill and Japanese ability in a designated industry, with a longer stay and, in time, the possibility of family status.

ROUTE B

Technical intern trainee

A structured programme of work and training, with an approved sending organisation in Pakistan and a supervising company in Japan.

AFTER ARRIVAL

Required support

As a registered support organisation we provide the orientation, interpretation, housing help and regular contact the law requires of us.

The industries

Where the jobs are

  • Food service: kitchens, service and store work
  • Food and beverage manufacturing
  • Agriculture and livestock
  • Fisheries and aquaculture
  • Accommodation: hotels and inns
  • Aviation ground handling
  • Automobile repair and maintenance
  • Shipbuilding and ship machinery
  • Construction
  • Electrical, electronics and information
Before you apply

What has to be in place

  • The age and health requirements for the industry
  • Japanese at the level the route requires, proven by a recognised test
  • Passport, and a clear record for the visa interview
  • Skills evidence or experience for the skilled route
  • Willingness to work under Japanese workplace rules

Requirements differ by industry and change with Japanese policy. We confirm the current rule for your case in writing, before you spend money on anything.

The examination

STBJ, and why it counts

The STBJ measures business Japanese from the basic level. It is recognised by Japan's Immigration Bureau as proof of proficiency for admission to language education institutions: 350 points or higher is treated as A1 or above on the Japanese-language education reference framework, and as equivalent to A2 for the notification standards.

Disclaimer

What we do not promise.

Nothing on this site is a promise of a visa, a job, a salary or a date. Every route to Japan is decided by Japanese employers and by the immigration authorities, not by us. A placement exists only when a contract exists.

Language proficiency is a condition of the routes offered. Without a recognised certificate you are not yet eligible to travel under them, and no payment changes that.

Fees, examination charges and travel costs are explained in writing, item by item, before anything is paid, and no fee is taken for work that has not started.

Working hours, wages and living arrangements are set by the employer and by Japanese law, and are given to you in the contract before travel. If anything is unclear, ask in writing, and keep the answer.
